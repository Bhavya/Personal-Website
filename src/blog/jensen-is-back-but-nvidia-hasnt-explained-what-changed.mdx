Nvidia’s board chair Harvey Jones told investors on November 17 that the board didn’t intend to bring Jensen Huang back as CEO. By November 23, directors had unanimously decided to do exactly that. I’m not particularly surprised that they wanted him back, but the explanation requires a bit more than another statement of confidence in him.

Replacing founders whose technical judgment and relationships are closely tied to their companies is difficult, and pretending Jensen is easily replaceable doesn’t make the decision any more responsible. Keeping him might genuinely be the better choice for Nvidia. I’m less clear on how the board reconciled that choice with what it had told everyone six days earlier.

Jensen [**acknowledged being unfaithful in his November 8 message to employees**](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/a-message-to-our-company), and the board separately [**opened an independent investigation into reports that he had been targeted by a foreign intelligence effort**](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-nvidia-board-of-directors). It said at the time that it had no reason to believe national security or Nvidia secrets had been compromised. The reports are the basis for an investigation, and they shouldn’t be treated as findings that he disclosed company secrets.

Nvidia [**announced his resignation on November 12**](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition), appointed Colette Kress interim CEO and started looking for a permanent replacement. On the [**November 17 earnings call**](https://s201.q4cdn.com/141608511/files/content_files/NVDA-3Q27-Earnings-Transcript.pdf), Jones connected the resignation to personal behaviour inconsistent with the company’s code of conduct and standards. He also said the board had retained a search firm, so this wasn’t presented as Jensen briefly stepping aside while directors figured things out.

***The national security explanation***

In its [**November 23 announcement**](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-board-reinstates-jensen-huang-as-president-ceo-and-board-director), the board says discussions with Jensen satisfied directors about his candour and ability to lead. It also says input from senior levels of the US government gave them confidence that bringing him back posed no national security risk, although the independent investigation is continuing.

Directors may have received information they can’t publish, and I don’t expect Nvidia to disclose sensitive intelligence or provide a running commentary on Jensen’s marriage. Waiting for every question to be resolved isn’t always practical either, but the company still has room to explain why the conduct Jones described last week no longer prevents Jensen from leading it.

Perhaps directors concluded that additional oversight was enough, or that the consequences of replacing him outweighed their concerns. Either is a decision they could explain without sharing anyone’s private life. Reassurance about national security, by itself, doesn’t explain how they reassessed conduct they had already described as inconsistent with Nvidia’s own standards.

***What happens if the board disagrees with him again?***

Nvidia has announced an independent board committee for national security and export controls, and it’s looking for a chief compliance officer who will report to the board’s governance committee. Those are substantive arrangements, and I’m glad the company isn’t presenting his return as a reason to abandon the investigation.

Whether those arrangements work depends on what happens when the people responsible for oversight disagree with him. If compliance objects to something commercially valuable, I want to know who has the authority to stop it and how disagreements reach the board. Creating the roles doesn’t tell us how those decisions will be made.

Honestly, it’s entirely possible to believe Jensen is the best person to run Nvidia and still want a much better explanation of the conditions under which he’s returning. His value to the company makes retaining him understandable, but it also makes challenging him more difficult. If the investigation eventually reaches conclusions that make keeping him uncomfortable, the board will have to make another decision, and I want to know what it has committed to doing differently this time.
