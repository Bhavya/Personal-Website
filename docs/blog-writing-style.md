# Blog writing style

Bhavya selected `src/blog/the-web-is-becoming-an-unstable-dependency-for-ai-assistants.mdx` as the authoritative reference for her article voice and formatting on October 16, 2026. Read its current contents before drafting or revising posts.

## Formatting

- Keep the frontmatter title as the page title.
- Within the article, use standalone bold-italic paragraph labels: `***Section label***`. Do not use Markdown headings (`##`, `###`, etc.) for those labels.
- Separate labels and paragraphs with blank lines.
- Bold the descriptive text of inline source links: `[**descriptive source text**](https://...)`.
- Use connected paragraphs with natural variation in length. Short paragraphs should serve the thought, not manufacture drama.
- Preserve existing dates, slugs, tags, images, and source URLs when editing unless the requested change requires otherwise.

## Voice and reasoning

- Write as a thoughtful person explaining what she noticed and how she reached her view. State the concrete observation, explain why it matters, then develop the implication.
- Use plain, direct, conversational language and contractions where natural. The reference mixes contractions and full forms; do not mechanically enforce either.
- First-person judgments and occasional understated asides are welcome when they fit. Never invent an experience, job responsibility, anecdote, or feeling.
- Give technical terms enough concrete explanation for the intended reader. Retain technical precision.
- Let section labels describe the next thought naturally. Avoid a report-like taxonomy or generic labels such as "Key takeaways."
- Avoid punchy marketing copy, aphorisms, forced hooks, slogan-like contrasts, dramatic fragments, polished mic-drop endings, and generic thought leadership.
- Do not force slang, jokes, "lol," personal anecdotes, or uncertainty into every post.
- State opinions confidently. Use uncertainty when it is real and specific, not as a stock softener ("I'm curious," "it will be interesting to see").
- Do not use em dashes. Keep sentences readable and avoid packing several logical steps into one sentence.
- Preserve nuance and show the causal steps. A reported concentration in VC fundraising, for example, does not establish that useful businesses lack all financing options.
- Link to sources close to the relevant claim. Verify new factual claims; distinguish evidence from the author's interpretation.
- End when the argument is complete. A genuine unresolved question can work; a formulaic engagement question is not required.

The reference is a style model, not a source of reusable personal anecdotes or facts for unrelated posts.

## Opinion articles

- Lead with a clear, contestable position and the decision it should change. Explain why the issue deserves attention now, with concrete stakes and a supported reason delay matters.
- Use examples and technical mechanics to support the argument. Do not substitute a workflow tutorial or a list of sensible practices for an opinion.
- Urgency must come from real consequences, constraints, developments, or accumulating costs. Do not manufacture deadlines, claim widespread failures without evidence, or use alarmist marketing language.
- Keep the author's position visible throughout, including the conclusion. Prefer clear reasoning over repeated 'I would want' qualifications.
- Bhavya generally does not start sentences with conjunctions.
